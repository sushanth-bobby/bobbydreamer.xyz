# bobbydreamer.xyz content architecture

This is the permanent editorial and knowledge-organization authority for the site. `AUTHORING.md` explains how to apply it; `CUSTOMIZATIONS.md` explains the Quartz implementation; historical phase reports contain detailed evidence.

## Principles

- The site is a long-running personal learning, building, and research journal.
- Historical writing is preserved. Old technical instructions are not silently rewritten as though they were written today.
- Numeric sequence is authorial chronology. Frontmatter date is calendar chronology. They are deliberately independent.
- Tags are fine-grained metadata. Topics are a small curated discovery layer; they are not a second copy of every tag.
- Content stays in self-contained folders with its assets.
- AI assistance does not transfer authorship away from the user when the project, experience, observations, conclusions, and editorial control remain the user's.
- Investment writing records personal investigation and learning. It is not a stock-tip or recommendation service.

## Content kinds

| Kind                   | Source and route                                                      | Contract                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Blog Article           | `content/<number>-<slug>/index.md` or a retained dotted-number folder | A valid date selects Blog Article presentation. A leading numeric sequence additionally places it in Latest, Blog order, RSS, and Previous/Next. |
| Home                   | `content/index.md` → `/`                                              | Authored introduction and Working on links, plus generated Latest Articles and Graph View.                                                       |
| Static pages           | `bio`, `irevere`, `music`, and `til`                                  | Stable non-article routes with page-specific composition.                                                                                        |
| Blog and year archives | Generated `/blog/` and `/blog/<year>/`                                | All articles use numeric order; year pages use date membership and date-descending order.                                                        |
| Topics                 | Generated `/topics/`                                                  | Curated groups of existing tag routes.                                                                                                           |
| Tags                   | Generated `/tags/` and `/tags/<tag>`                                  | Complete fine-grained taxonomy and per-tag listings.                                                                                             |

The 2026-09-28 audit contains 155 Markdown documents: 150 Blog Articles, one home page, and four static pages. Of those, 153 are deterministic Gatsby migrations and two are authored directly in Quartz. Snapshot counts may grow; the contracts above do not depend on a fixed count.

## Provenance and historical preservation

`GatsbyMigration/content-original` is immutable migration source. The 153 migration-owned destination folders are regenerated by `scripts/migrate-gatsby.mjs`; they are not ordinary hand-edit targets.

Approved editorial metadata for migrated articles belongs in `scripts/content-editorial-overrides.json`. The migration applies that ledger deterministically, so a future rebuild retains status, specific Learning Archive context, and authorship decisions without altering source prose. Newly authored Quartz articles keep those fields in their own frontmatter.

Do not modernize commands, package versions, screenshots, conclusions, or investment observations inside historical articles merely to make them current. A genuinely new treatment should normally be a new numbered article linked from the older one.

## Status model

Omitting `status` means `current`. Use an explicit status only after article-level review.

| Status          | Meaning                                                                                     | Reader presentation                                            |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `historical`    | A technical learning record whose versions, tools, commands, or practices may have changed. | Central Learning Archive notice.                               |
| `superseded`    | A newer article or approach replaces this one.                                              | Central Superseded article notice and required successor link. |
| `point-in-time` | Intentionally records observations, decisions, or circumstances at a particular time.       | Central Point-in-time journal notice.                          |

`superseded` requires `supersededBy` with the successor's canonical site path. The centralized component `@bdv/quartz-content-context` owns all reader-facing wording.

The technical Learning Archive preserves what the user was learning, using, trying, and building at the time. It is not a promise that old Python, Java, Node.js, Pandas, Firebase, Gatsby, Google Cloud, Db2, web, API, library, or framework material remains compatible with current releases. Do not modernize the original prose, code, commands, or screenshots. The standard reader notice is:

> Learning archive: This article reflects my experience with the technologies and versions available when it was written. Some technical details may have changed since then.

The approved Phase 6.1 ledger assigns `historical` to 90 substantively technical learning articles. Optional `learningArchiveContexts` values add concise Gatsby-era, Google Domains, or `gsutil` context within the same notice; they do not create another status system. Multiple specific contexts still render as one coherent presentation.

Classification is based on article purpose, not age or keyword occurrence. New technical articles remain current by default. Investment journals, personal writing, general notes, and historical decisions are not Learning Archive material merely because they mention code or a product. An investment analysis whose substantive purpose is a Pandas/NumPy experiment may qualify; an investment journal that only embeds a Python-generated chart does not. Point-in-time records remain distinct, and `superseded` still requires a real successor article.

## Authorship and AI assistance

The default, with no `authorship` field, is user-authored. Research, outlining, drafting, editing, rewriting, code help, source discovery, grammar assistance, and fact checking by AI do not change that when the underlying work and editorial control belong to the user.

Use:

```yaml
authorship: ai
```

only when an article is intentionally substantially investigated and authored by AI. The site then renders `Written by AI`. Do not use `wai` as a tag; authorship is provenance, not taxonomy.

## Tags and Topics

Tags describe individual articles and may remain historically specific. Preserve useful old labels rather than forcing a cosmetically uniform taxonomy. New tags should be lowercase kebab-case, singular where natural, and reusable. Numeric-only tags and `wai` are invalid.

Topics are curated entry points backed by existing tag routes. The current landing page groups the established eleven curated tags without changing their routes:

- Build & technology: web development, GCP, Python, Node.js, Gatsby, Firebase, JavaScript, and Pandas.
- Investment learning & journal: Quest for Wealth.
- Learning & reflection: notes and personal development.

The conceptual investment area has four editorial subtypes:

1. Concepts — valuation, owner earnings, ratios, risk, market concepts, and frameworks.
2. Research & Analysis — personal company, sector, market, result, and decision journals.
3. Tools & Experiments — screeners, data pipelines, Python analysis, and future Kickbear analytical work.
4. Investors & Books — investors, books, letters, interviews, frameworks, and personal takeaways.

These are editorial distinctions, not mandatory physical folders or top-navigation links. Add a separate curated route only when enough content makes it useful.

## Investment journal boundary

Investment articles may discuss companies, holdings, screens, models, personal buy/sell/reject decisions, later outcomes, and lessons. Their purpose is to document:

> what I investigated, why I investigated it, what I observed, what I decided for myself, what subsequently happened, and what I learned.

They must not be framed as tips, individualized financial advice, guaranteed outcomes, or instructions for the reader to trade. A point-in-time market observation remains valid historical journal material without an outdated-content warning.

## Knowledge relationships

Use the smallest relationship that helps a reader:

- normal Markdown links for related or continued reading;
- a shared tag for a genuine series or recurring subject;
- numeric Previous/Next for authorial chronology;
- `supersededBy` only when a newer article replaces an older one;
- native backlinks and Graph View for emergent relationships.

Do not introduce a general relationship database, duplicate manual related-article lists everywhere, or reconstruct Kickbear Journaling inside this site. The architecture can publish future Kickbear articles as normal numbered folders with appropriate tags and links.

## Corpus audit

`scripts/audit-content.mjs` inventories every Markdown document and validates permanent editorial metadata. Its machine-readable output includes sequence, canonical path, title, description, date, tags, page/content type, provenance, year, age, word count, headings, TOC eligibility, images, colocated assets, internal/external links, code blocks, technology/product references, topic signals, status, relationships, and authorship.

Run:

```powershell
npm run validate:content
node scripts/audit-content.mjs --as-of YYYY-MM-DD --json "<report-path>"
```

The dated Phase 6 inventory and audit reports live in `bdv4q2-codex-todo`; they are evidence, not public content.

## Non-goals

This architecture does not add a CMS, database, user workflow, recommendation engine, stock-tip section, automated mass tagging, external image service, or AI publishing backend. It does not rename routes, renumber posts, move assets, or replace the migration pipeline.
